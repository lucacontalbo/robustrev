\subsection{Extended discussion and reiteration of core design choices and findings}
The material presented in the main body of the paper and in the appendices captures the technical construction, experimental protocol, and empirical observations that motivated the design of \name{}. In this subsection we restate, expand upon, and connect those points in greater detail. Our goal is not to introduce novel empirical results or additional quantitative claims, but rather to provide a more discursive, explicit narrative that emphasizes the motivations behind particular design decisions, clarifies the intended uses of the generator, and synthesizes the practical implications of the experimental findings reported earlier. This expanded discussion is intended to make the paper's arguments and trade-offs more accessible to readers who prefer a slower, more discursive treatment of the material, and to surface the implicit rationales that link the pipeline's components to the behaviors observed in the evaluation.

Decoupling supervision from presentation. A fundamental design choice of \name{} is the strict separation between a latent relational source (used exclusively for computing and verifying gold answers) and the perturbed, non-relational views shown to evaluated models. Reiterating this point in plain terms: by constructing a canonical, executable representation of the data and then deriving all evaluated renderings from that canonical source, we obtain two complementary benefits. First, we preserve verifiability—every gold label stems from an executable SQL expression over a single, coherent representation—so we can guarantee that the nominal answer is correct even after aggressive presentation perturbations are applied. Second, we gain fine-grained experimental control: because the renderings are derived transformations rather than independent artifacts, we can systematically vary only particular dimensions of interest (layout, unit rendering, merged headers, typographical noise, etc.) while holding the underlying evidence and the SQL program fixed. This architectural decoupling is central to the paper's ability to attribute performance differences to presentation and evidence-distribution factors rather than to differences in the underlying facts or the answer keys themselves.

Why a from-scratch generator? The decision to generate latent sources from scratch rather than to start from existing relational databases is worth restating carefully. Existing relational benchmarks are invaluable for many research goals, but they come with distributional and structural idiosyncrasies that limit their usefulness when the evaluation objective is to exercise specific non-relational phenomena at scale. In particular, converting arbitrary relational tables into dense, hierarchical, pivoted views often fails: many real-world tables do not exhibit the dense combinatorial structure required to produce plausible hierarchical headers, and attempting to force such transformations can produce unrealistic or degenerate renderings. By generating the latent source ourselves, \name{} guarantees the presence of the combinations, convertible units, and compatible cross-table joins needed for the non-relational scenarios we aim to stress-test. This choice trades off absolute realism in the distributional sense—synthetic data is not a perfect mirror of all real documents—in favor of reproducible, interpretable, and controllable diagnostics that isolate specific failure modes.

Two evidence-flow topologies and why they matter. A second core element of the generator is the explicit modeling of two evidence-flow topologies, which we call parallel and sequential. Parallel instances capture the common analytics pattern in which evidence is distributed across multiple tables and the final answer is an aggregation of independently retrieved scalars. Sequential instances capture a complementary family of tasks where intermediate retrievals produce lookup keys that must be propagated to subsequent tables before a final aggregation is possible. These two topologies differ in the cognitive and algorithmic skills they demand from a model: parallel tasks emphasize robust retrieval and consistent normalization (for instance, unit alignment across views), whereas sequential tasks emphasize reliable key recovery and cross-view linking. Restating this dichotomy clarifies why performance degrades along different axes in the two regimes and why both must be considered when assessing systems intended for real-world analytical workflows.

Rendering perturbations: what we perturb and why. The rendering stage implements a compact but expressive set of perturbations: pivots into hierarchical headers, merged cells, blank rows and columns, typographical noise, units rendered in varying surface forms and locations, and contextual/footnote-like text injected into cells. It is important to emphasize the functional reason for each perturbation. Hierarchical headers and pivoting remove the simple one-row-per-record presentation that many relational QA approaches assume; merged cells and blank rows are common in human-authored spreadsheets and break naive assumptions about cell alignment; typographical noise and inline textual context mimic common authoring practices where numbers are accompanied by explanatory strings or abbreviations; and unit heterogeneity reflects the frequency with which compatible quantities are reported in different units across tables or within the same report. Each perturbation is applied in an answer-preserving manner because we maintain cell-level provenance masks during transformation; this ensures that despite perturbations the gold label remains verifiable. Re-emphasizing these motivations helps readers understand that the chosen perturbations are not arbitrary decorations but targeted interventions meant to probe known brittlenesses in tabular reasoning and retrieval.

Lightweight semantic constraints and optional user guidance. The pipeline includes an optional semantic-constraint stage that infers (or accepts user-specified) intra-row and inter-row regularities to steer sampling toward plausible table instantiations. The rationale is pragmatic: unconstrained sampling can produce semantically implausible combinations that, while technically valid, may reduce the interpretability of generated scenarios. The constraints we use are deliberately lightweight and expressive enough to model common relationships (for example, ordering relationships between ``gross'' and ``net'' values or simple conditional bounds) while preserving randomness in the sampled values. When higher realism is required, the semi-automatic mode allows practitioners to inspect and edit constraints before instantiation. This design acknowledges that plausibility is sometimes a domain-specific judgment best supplied by human experts, while preserving the generator's default automated operation for rapid experimentation.

Question verbalization and verification as the remaining human-in-the-loop. In our pipeline, the only stage that cannot be guaranteed correct by construction is the natural-language verbalization of the SQL program. For that reason, \name{} includes an automated verification and repair pass that attempts to identify mismatches between the verbalization and the latent supervision, and we also performed manual spot checks to estimate fidelity. Restating this pipeline design underscores a pragmatic truth: while answers and evidence provenance can be mechanically guaranteed, the process of producing human-like, unambiguous natural-language questions typically benefits from LLM-based generation plus verification. The verification step is therefore not a mere afterthought but an integral part of ensuring that the resulting benchmark instances are coherent and faithful to the intended SQL semantics.

Qualitative synthesis of experimental observations. The experiments in the main paper show that increasing table count, introducing non-relational formatting, and injecting unit heterogeneity produce compounded difficulty for contemporary LLMs. Restating these observations qualitatively: as the number of independent evidence sources grows, models are more likely to fail either in locating all required evidence entries or in aggregating them correctly; when presentation obfuscates the relational structure (for example, via hierarchical headers or merged cells), models are less able to map the observable layout back to an underlying schema-like representation; and when units differ across evidence pieces, models often fail to perform consistent normalization prior to aggregation. Importantly, these effects interact: unit issues and layout-induced retrieval failures do not simply additively hurt performance but often multiply the difficulty by introducing ambiguous or inconsistent inputs that complicate both retrieval and arithmetic. Reiterating these qualitative patterns without re-stating specific numeric drops helps highlight the conceptual takeaways for practitioners.

On the utility of \name{} as a training augmentation. While the generator was designed primarily as a diagnostic and evaluation tool, the experiments also show that its outputs can be used as additional training supervision to improve cross-table performance in scarce-data regimes. Reiterating the practical implication: synthetic, controllable multi-table examples can complement small, curated in-domain datasets and reduce brittleness on the very multi-table phenomena that are rare or absent in existing corpora. This complements, rather than replaces, the need for carefully curated benchmarks that reflect the idiosyncrasies of specific applied settings.

Limitations and appropriate scope of use. It is worth restating our position on the scope and appropriate use of \name. The generator is designed as a controlled probe and a means to create scalable multi-table supervision; it is not a substitute for large-scale, curated, real-world corpora when those are available. Synthetic realism is necessarily limited: no automatic generator can yet capture the full distribution of visual, rhetorical, and domain-specific idiosyncrasies found in naturally occurring reports and spreadsheets. Consequently, strong performance on our synthetic benchmarks should be interpreted as evidence of progress on the specific failure modes we encode (multi-table retrieval, non-relational presentation handling, and unit normalization), but not as a blanket assurance of real-world robustness. Conversely, poor performance on \name{} pinpoints concrete areas of weakness that merit targeted engineering and data-collection efforts.

Best practices for practitioners. Finally, for researchers and practitioners who intend to use \name{}, we summarize recommended practices consistent with the generator's design goals. Use the latent-source decoupling to construct ablations that isolate specific layout or unit effects; when moving toward deployment, supplement synthetic \name{} examples with a small curated set of real examples to capture visual or domain-specific phenomena not modeled by the generator; when higher plausibility is important, take advantage of the semi-automatic constraint interface so that domain experts can inject known regularities; and treat \name{}-derived training data as a complement to, not a replacement for, careful in-domain annotation. These recommendations are aligned with the overall message of the paper: controlled synthetic generation is a powerful diagnostic and data-augmentation tool, but its outputs must be interpreted and used thoughtfully within the broader pipeline of model development and evaluation.

Concluding restatement. In short, \name{}'s value lies principally in three related capabilities that we have emphasized throughout: (i) the ability to preserve verifiability by computing answers on a canonical latent source while presenting perturbed, non-relational views for evaluation; (ii) the facility to control and vary the number of tables, the evidence-flow topology, and a targeted suite of structural and numerical perturbations; and (iii) the practical utility of producing scalable examples that both reveal model blind spots and supply additional multi-table supervision. The detailed experimental evidence and the appendices document how these capabilities manifest in practice; the extended discussion above is intended to make the underlying reasoning transparent and to help readers apply \name{} to their own diagnostic and training needs in a principled way.
